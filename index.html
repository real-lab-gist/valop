VaLoP

Value Gradient Lookahead and Pullback for Reward-Guided Diffusion

Kwanyoung KimDepartment of AI, GIST
Paper soon arXiv soon Code soon BibTeX

One guidance rule for image generators and robot policies

Abstract

Value-gradient guidance is least accurate at high noise, where a sample commits to a mode. VaLoP reads the value one step ahead and pulls its gradient back, and VaLoP-Fast replaces the pullback with a lightweight head.

Reward guidance steers a pretrained diffusion or flow model toward outputs that a reward model prefers, at sampling time and without retraining the model for each reward. It follows the gradient of a value function, the expected reward given the noisy state. The two common estimates of this gradient, gradients through a one-step denoised estimate and learned value models, are least accurate at high noise, where the coarse structure of the sample is decided. Interestingly, we find that both errors are posterior covariances, which grow with the uncertainty about the clean sample, and hence at higher noise.

To reduce them, we evaluate the value at a less noisy state, one sampler step ahead, where this uncertainty is smaller, and pull its gradient back to the current state. This lookahead places both estimators at the two ends of one family and reveals that an intermediate lookahead requires one additional term, a correction through the generator. We therefore propose Value Gradient Lookahead and Pullback (VaLoP), which computes this correction with one vector-Jacobian product, and VaLoP-Fast, which amortizes it with a lightweight head that, for text-to-image models, is trained with a single reward and reused for the others.

Across various models, rewards, and benchmark datasets, both robustly outperform existing value-gradient guidance on the target reward, VaLoP-Fast at about the cost of learned-value guidance. VaLoP-Fast also attains the highest success rates in critic guidance of robot policies on LIBERO and RoboCasa.

Why it matters

Errors grow with noise

Both common value-gradient estimates, DPS and a learned value model (StitchVM), err by posterior covariances that grow with noise. VaLoP reads the value one step ahead, where the clean sample is less uncertain, and pulls the gradient back.

At scale: one guided step on SD3.5-Medium

StitchVM's HPSv2 gain per step drops by more than half at high noise. Read one step ahead, the same value model gains 2.1× as much at t = 0.6–0.8.

Method

Look ahead, then pull back

1

Look ahead

zs = zt − (t − s) vθ(zt, t)

One sampler step to a less noisy state, at s = 0.6t.

2

Read the value there

gs = ∇Vψ(zs, s)

The value model is far more accurate here than at zt.

3

Pull it back

gt = Ps⊤gs = gs + ξt

ξt costs one VJP through the generator in VaLoP and one head pass in VaLoP-Fast.

4

Guide the current state

zt−Δt = zt − Δt vθ + η gt

Step toward the rewarded mode, with the generator and value model frozen.

At s = t this is learned-value guidance, and at s = 0 the DPS form. In between, one extra term appears, the correction through the generator.
